1. Introduction

Cybersecurity breaches have become one of the most serious threats facing organizations and individuals today. A cybersecurity breach occurs when unauthorized individuals gain access to confidential systems, networks, or data. These attacks can lead to data theft, financial loss, service disruption, and reputational damage.

This report examines recent cybersecurity breaches, their causes, and their impacts. It also includes a simulation exercise focused on identifying phishing emails using online tools.

2. Recent Cybersecurity Breaches
2.1 Snowflake Data Breach (2024)

The Snowflake data breach involved unauthorized access to cloud environments hosted on the Snowflake platform, affecting over 100 organizations. Attackers used stolen login credentials to access sensitive data stored by various companies.

Cause

Stolen credentials from users

Lack of strong authentication controls

Poor security monitoring

Impact

Exposure of sensitive customer information

Financial and reputational damage to affected companies

Increased concern about cloud security risks

2.2 ManageMyHealth Data Breach (2025)

The ManageMyHealth patient portal breach involved unauthorized access to a healthcare platform in New Zealand. Hackers extracted more than 400,000 medical documents belonging to over 120,000 patients.

Cause

Unauthorized system access

Weak security controls in the online patient portal

Impact

Exposure of highly sensitive medical records

Privacy risks for patients

Government investigations and legal actions

2.3 Kadokawa / Niconico Ransomware Attack (2024)

The Japanese media company Kadokawa and its video platform Niconico experienced a ransomware attack that disrupted services for weeks. Around 254,000 users’ personal data was leaked.

Cause

A phishing attack targeting employees

Malware deployed by attackers

Impact

Website shutdown and operational disruption

Leakage of user data

Financial losses and system rebuilding costs

3. Common Causes of Cybersecurity Breaches

From the cases above, several major causes of cyber breaches can be identified:

3.1 Phishing Attacks

Phishing emails trick users into revealing sensitive information or installing malicious software. Attackers often impersonate trusted organizations.

3.2 Weak Passwords or Credential Theft

Hackers often use stolen usernames and passwords to access systems without triggering security alerts.

3.3 Poor Security Configuration

Systems that lack proper updates, monitoring, or authentication controls are easier targets for attackers.

3.4 Supply Chain Vulnerabilities

Attackers may compromise third-party vendors or software updates to gain access to larger organizations.

4. Phishing Email Identification Simulation

To understand phishing attacks better, I used free online phishing simulation and training tools to analyze suspicious emails.

Common Characteristics of Phishing Emails
1. Suspicious Sender Address

The email address often looks similar to a legitimate company but contains small spelling changes.

Example:
support@paypa1.com
 instead of support@paypal.com

2. Urgent or Threatening Language

Phishing emails often create urgency such as:

“Your account will be suspended immediately”

“Verify your account within 24 hours”

Attackers use urgency and fear to pressure victims into acting quickly.

3. Suspicious Links

Links may redirect users to fake websites designed to steal login credentials.

4. Requests for Sensitive Information

Legitimate companies rarely ask for passwords, banking details, or personal information via email.

5. Poor Grammar or Formatting

Many phishing emails contain spelling errors or unusual formatting.

5. Tools Used for Phishing Identification

The following free tools can be used to analyze suspicious emails:

Google Safe Browsing

VirusTotal

PhishTank

MXToolbox

These tools help verify links, domains, and email headers to determine if a message is malicious.

6. Conclusion

Cybersecurity breaches continue to increase globally, affecting organizations across many sectors including healthcare, finance, and technology. Most breaches occur due to phishing attacks, weak authentication, and poor system security.

Organizations must adopt stronger security measures such as employee training, multi-factor authentication, regular system updates, and continuous monitoring to reduce the risk of cyberattacks.
